Our Gen X Life Research phase 16 Aug 2026

Nostalgia Desk

A weekly bot that reads the internet for Keith and Abby — video ideas, merch ideas, and a list of what to grab at Saturday's estate sale. This is the research: which numbers are worth watching, and which doors are actually open.

The instinct is to point a bot at what's trending right now. That doesn't work here. The fix is to invert it: you supply the vocabulary, the bot measures the movement.

01 Why general trend feeds fail this brand

Two facts settle the architecture before any code gets written.

Google Trends' daily list is news. Elections, sports scores, celebrity deaths, last night's Netflix drop. It refreshes hourly and it will never once surface a Trapper Keeper.

YouTube's "most popular" chart is no longer general. In July 2025 YouTube changed what that endpoint returns — it now feeds from the Trending Music, Movies and Gaming charts. The first endpoint anyone reaches for is now structurally incapable of showing a nostalgia channel.

So instead: a watchlist of a few hundred Gen X terms — objects, brands, shows, snacks — measured every week against their own 90-day baseline. Not what's big, but what moved. "Fisher-Price Little People up 240% week over week" is a video idea. "Star Wars is popular" is not.

A second layer reads the nostalgia subreddits and surfaces terms that are spiking but aren't on the watchlist yet. That's the half that finds things before you do.

One consequence to plan around: this bot is worth nothing in week one. Every metric is relative to a baseline it has to build first. It gets genuinely useful around week four to six. Anything it says before then is noise.

03 The sources, in build order

Tier 1 · free, no gatesWikipedia pageviews — the sleeper pick

No key, no meaningful rate limit, no terms-of-service exposure, and near-perfect for this brand specifically: every Gen X artifact has an article. Teddy Ruxpin. Zima. Blockbuster. Pyrex. The metric is daily views scored against that article's own 90-day mean — a spike means something reminded a lot of people of that object this week. Cheapest real signal in the list, and almost nobody uses it this way.

Tier 1YouTube Data API — but not the obvious endpoint

Free, 10,000 units a day. The catch: a search costs 100 units, so the real budget is about 100 searches a day, not 10,000. That's why the watchlist cycles across the week instead of hitting everything daily. Fetching stats for videos you've already found is nearly free — 50 at a time for a single unit.

The metric that matters

outlier = (views ÷ days since publish) ÷ channel subscribers

A 12k-subscriber channel pulling 300k views in two weeks proves the topic is hungry and that you don't need a big channel to catch it. Cross it with how many videos on that topic exist — high demand against low supply is the gap you film into.

The part worth paying for isn't the trend line — it's rising related queries. Google flags anything growing past 5000% as "Breakout." That's a purpose-built discovery mechanism and the most direct route to "this is about to be a video" anywhere in this document. Build it so nothing breaks when Trends is unavailable; treat it as a bonus, not a spine.

Tier 1 · apply in week oneReddit — the discovery layer

Free for non-commercial use at 100 queries a minute. But new app registration now goes through manual review rather than self-service, and the unauthenticated endpoints free tools relied on started refusing requests in May 2026. The credential is the long pole on this entire project — apply now even though it's the fourth thing on the list.

Two metrics. Top posts of the week is the obvious one. The interesting one is noun frequency against a trailing baseline: when "Caboodles" starts appearing in r/GenX four times as often as its 90-day average, that's a term to promote — and nobody had to think of it. Watch r/whatisthisthing and r/ThriftStoreHauls for Abby especially; they're a live feed of objects people found and cared enough to ask about.

Tier 2 · the money layereBay — and the sold-comps problem

This is the one place where the obvious answer genuinely isn't available:

WhatGives youStatus
findCompletedItemsSold pricesShut off Feb 2025
Marketplace Insights90 days soldBusiness-only
Browse APIActive listingsFree, 5k/day
Terapeak1 year soldFree w/ seller acct

Browse gives asking prices and listing counts — not what things sell for, and any tool that blurs that is lying to you. But it still earns its place: rising asking prices against falling listing counts means supply is tightening, which is a real buy signal without sold data.

For actual sold prices the practical answer isn't an API at all. Terapeak comes free with an eBay seller account, which Abby likely already has. The bot narrows the field to ten objects a week; Terapeak confirms them by hand in five minutes. That division of labour beats a worse automated answer.

Tier 2Etsy — small scope, but it's the scoreboard

No market-wide trending endpoint exists. But seller access approves in minutes and gives full data on her own shop — views, favourites and sales per listing. That isn't discovery, it's something arguably more useful: the feedback loop. Check the bot's predictions against what actually sold, then weight the sources that earn it. A trend bot with no scoreboard drifts.

Tier 2 · recommend againstEstate sale listings

EstateSales.net has no public API and its robots.txt disallows a number of paths. Scraping it is the greyest thing in this document for the least return — and Abby doesn't need a list of sales. She needs to know what to look for once she's already standing there:

BUTTERPRINT PYREX — asking +30% this month, listings −18%,
3 front-page ThriftStoreHauls posts this week.
Target buy under $15 · comps $45–70

That's built entirely from Tier 1 and 2 data, with no scraping exposure at all.

04 How a topic gets scored

Raw movement isn't enough — things spike for useless reasons. Five factors, and the last two are what keep the report true for this brand rather than just true.

FactorThe question
MomentumMoving now, against its own baseline?
Volume floorIs anyone looking at all? Kills noise.
CompetitionHow crowded is it already?
Cohort fitGen X nostalgia, or Gen Z's?
MonetizableCan it be a shirt, or a findable object?

Cohort fit earns its own note. A lot of what's "retro" right now is Y2K and early-2000s — that's Gen Z's nostalgia, not this audience's. Without that filter the bot will confidently recommend filming about things your audience has no memory of. One rule that prevents a systematic failure.

05 What lands Monday morning

Film this — 5 ideas

Topic, why it moved, the numbers behind it, the best recent video on it and how small that channel was, and a suggested title.

Print this — 3 concepts

Phrase or object, the momentum behind it, and whether it's trademark-clear.

Grab this — 10 objects

Object, target buy price, comp range, direction of travel, confidence.

New terms — yes / no

What Reddit surfaced that isn't on the watchlist yet. Ten seconds a week of human input; it's the bot getting smarter.

Emailed, and archived at a private page on the site. No dashboard to remember to log into.

06 Build shape and cost

Nothing here needs new infrastructure. A scheduled job in the repo that already exists, TypeScript to match the site, and a small local database — history is mandatory, because every metric is relative. Each source stays isolated, so one going down degrades the report instead of breaking it. Given the state of Google Trends, that isolation isn't optional.

08 Two things worth raising

The credential timeline is the schedule. Reddit's manual review is the long pole; everything else is fast. Applying for Reddit and the eBay keyset in week one means they land around when the code is ready instead of after it.

The bot makes ideas, not uploads. Your Life On Tape has been idle about two years and the podcast hasn't published since December 2024. A trend bot pointed at dormant channels produces a very good list of videos nobody makes. Worth deciding up front which surface this feeds — my read is the main channel and Abby's sourcing, with the merch list as a bonus rather than a third job.

→ Recommended next step

Build Tier 1 only — Wikipedia, YouTube, Reddit — against a 50-term seed watchlist, and let it collect for a month before trusting a word it says. Small build, costs nothing, proves the scoring model, and it starts the baseline clock immediately.

The baseline is the one thing you can't buy later. Every week of delay is a week the bot isn't smart yet.
